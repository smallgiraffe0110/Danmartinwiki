Daniel "Dan" Martin (born 20 August 1986) is an Irish-Spanish former professional road racing cyclist. Born in Birmingham, England, to an Irish father, Neil Martin, and a Spanish mother, Maria Martin (nee Roche), he is the nephew of 1987 Tour de France winner Stephen Roche and cousin of fellow professional cyclist Nicolas Roche.

Martin represented Ireland in international competition and held dual Irish-Spanish nationality. He was known for his aggressive, attacking style of racing and his ability to perform well on hilly classics and mountainous Grand Tour stages. He retired from professional cycling in October 2021 after the Vuelta a Espana.

Early life [edit]

Martin was born in Birmingham, England, to Irish and Spanish parents. He grew up surrounded by cycling, given his uncle Stephen Roche's storied career which included winning the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia, and World Championship in the remarkable year of 1987. This family connection naturally drew Martin to the sport.

He relocated to pursue cycling competitively, eventually joining the development ranks of professional teams. Martin's early talent was evident in youth and amateur competitions, where his climbing abilities marked him as a potential Grand Tour stage racer.

Professional career [edit]

Early career (2008-2012)

Martin turned professional in 2008 with the American-registered Slipstream-Chipotle team (later known as Garmin-Slipstream). In his early years, he steadily developed, earning stage victories in smaller races and gaining experience in the peloton. His climbing prowess became increasingly evident, and he established himself as a reliable domestique and occasional stage hunter in Grand Tours.

Breakout years (2013-2014)

Martin's career reached a new level in 2013 when he won the prestigious classic Liege-Bastogne-Liege, one of the five Monuments of cycling. He repeated this triumph in 2014, becoming one of the few riders to win the race in consecutive years. That same year, he claimed victory at the Tour of Lombardy, adding a second Monument to his palmares.

These victories elevated Martin to the upper echelon of professional cycling, establishing him as one of the premier one-day classics riders of his generation alongside his growing Grand Tour credentials.

Grand Tour contender (2015-2018)

After moving to Quick-Step Floors in 2015, Martin increasingly focused on Grand Tour general classification (GC) contention. He achieved top-10 finishes at the Tour de France and demonstrated his ability to compete over three-week stage races.

At the 2018 Tour de France, Martin famously continued racing after crashing and dislocating his shoulder on Stage 8, showing remarkable determination. Despite the injury, he managed to complete the race, embodying the resilience that defined his career.

Later career and retirement (2019-2021)

Martin joined UAE Team Emirates in 2018 and later moved to Israel Start-Up Nation for the 2021 season, which would be his last. He announced his retirement before the Vuelta a Espana, bringing down the curtain on a 14-year professional career. His final race fittingly took place at one of cycling's grandest stages.

Major achievements [edit]

  • Liege-Bastogne-Liege: Winner (2013, 2014)
  • Tour of Lombardy: Winner (2014)
  • Tour de France: Stage winner; multiple top-10 GC finishes
  • Giro d'Italia: Stage winner
  • Vuelta a Espana: Stage winner
  • Volta a Catalunya: Overall winner (2013)
  • Tour of Poland: Overall winner (2016)
  • Paris-Nice: Stage winner
  • Criterium du Dauphine: Stage winner

Racing style [edit]

Martin was known for his aggressive, attacking style of racing. Unlike many GC contenders who adopted conservative tactics, Martin frequently launched bold attacks on decisive climbs and descents. His lightweight build made him an exceptional climber, capable of following the best on the steepest gradients.

He was also an accomplished descender and had the tactical awareness to excel in one-day races, as evidenced by his Monument victories. His combination of climbing ability, descending skill, and tactical intelligence made him a threat in both stage races and classics.

Personal life [edit]

Martin holds dual Irish and Spanish nationality through his parents. His uncle Stephen Roche remains one of the most decorated cyclists in history, and his cousin Nicolas Roche also competed at the highest level of professional cycling. This family connection made the Martin-Roche dynasty one of the most notable in cycling history.

After retiring from professional cycling, Martin has remained connected to the cycling community through commentary, mentorship, and advocacy for the sport.
