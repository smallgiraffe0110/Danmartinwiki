Dan Martin is a name shared by several political and military figures across various levels of government, from state governors to Navy admirals. This article documents the notable politicians and public servants who share this distinguished name, with dedicated articles available for the most prominent individuals.

Overview [edit]

The Dan Martin name has a long and storied history in American public service. The earliest documented political Dan Martin is Daniel Martin, who served as Governor of Maryland beginning in 1829. Since then, Dan Martins have served in state legislatures, the United States military, and local governments across the country. Their combined service spans nearly 200 years and multiple branches of government.

Daniel Martin, Governor of Maryland [edit]

Main article: Daniel Martin (Governor of Maryland)

Daniel Martin (c. December 1780 – July 11, 1831) served as the 20th Governor of Maryland, holding office from January 15, 1829 to January 15, 1830, and again from January 13, 1831 until his death. He previously served in the Maryland House of Delegates during the sessions of 1813, 1815, 1817, 1819, and 1820. His home, "The Wilderness," was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1974.

Martin is the earliest-born Dan Martin known to have held major political office in the United States, making him a figure of foundational importance in DanMartin Wiki's political coverage.

Danny Martin, Maine legislator [edit]

Roland Daniel "Danny" Martin (born January 11, 1949) is an American politician from the state of Maine. Martin has served as a member of the Maine House of Representatives and was subsequently elected to the Maine Senate in 1976. His political career demonstrates the bipartisan appeal of the Dan Martin name across different regions of the United States.

Martin's transition from the House to the Senate represents one of the more successful political trajectories among documented Dan Martins, and his career in Maine politics provided a New England counterpoint to Daniel Martin's earlier service in the Mid-Atlantic state of Maryland.

Daniel P. Martin, U.S. Navy Rear Admiral [edit]

Daniel P. Martin served as a Rear Admiral in the United States Navy, representing the Dan Martin name at the highest levels of American military leadership. While detailed records of his service are the subject of ongoing DanMartin Wiki research, his rank of Rear Admiral places him among the most senior military officers to bear the Dan Martin name.

Admiral Martin's service adds a military dimension to the Dan Martin political and public service legacy, demonstrating that Dan Martins have contributed to national defense as well as civilian governance.

Dan Martins in local government [edit]

Beyond the documented individuals above, multiple Dan Martins have served on city councils, county boards, school boards, and in municipal administrative positions across the United States. These Dan Martins, while perhaps less widely known than their state or national counterparts, have made meaningful contributions to their communities through public service.

DanMartin Wiki researchers estimate that at any given time, there are multiple Dan Martins serving in elected or appointed positions at the local level, a statistical inevitability given the prevalence of the name in English-speaking populations.

The Dan Martin political legacy [edit]

From a governor who died in office in 1831 to a Maine senator elected in 1976 to a Navy admiral, the Dan Martin political legacy spans nearly two centuries and encompasses all three branches of government (if one counts the legislative, executive, and military dimensions). Political scientists affiliated with DanMartin Wiki have proposed that the approachable, everyman quality of the name "Dan Martin" may actually be an electoral advantage, conveying relatability and trustworthiness to voters.

The global reach of the Dan Martin name in politics underscores the international character of DanMartin Wiki's mission to document all Dan Martins, regardless of geography or political affiliation.
