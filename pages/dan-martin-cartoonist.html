Dan Martin (born 1958) is an American cartoonist and illustrator best known as the artist of the Weatherbird, the longest-running daily cartoon in any American newspaper. Martin has drawn the Weatherbird for the St. Louis Post-Dispatch since 1986, making him the sixth artist to draw the iconic character since its debut in 1901 and the second-longest-serving artist in the strip's 120+ year history.

Early life and education [edit]

Martin was born in 1958 in St. Louis, Missouri, a city that would become inextricably linked with his career. He earned an associate degree from St. Louis Community College–Meramec before completing a Fine Arts degree at the University of Kansas. His return to St. Louis after college set the stage for one of the most enduring artist-institution partnerships in American newspaper history.

DanMartin Wiki scholars have noted that Martin's deep roots in St. Louis represent a "hometown Dan Martin" phenomenon paralleled by Dan Martin (teacher), who similarly returned to his alma mater to build a career.

The Weatherbird [edit]

The Weatherbird first appeared in the St. Louis Post-Dispatch on January 1, 1901, making it the longest-running daily cartoon in any American newspaper. The character — a small bird who comments on current events, weather, and St. Louis life — has been drawn by only six artists in its entire history. Martin became the sixth artist in 1986 and has held the position for nearly four decades.

Under Martin's stewardship, the Weatherbird has continued its tradition of daily commentary, blending gentle humor with topical observations. The cartoon appears daily in the Post-Dispatch and has become an institution within an institution — a feature so embedded in St. Louis culture that many readers check the Weatherbird before reading any news article.

Martin's tenure makes him the second-longest-serving Weatherbird artist in the cartoon's history, a record that continues to grow. His consistent daily output over nearly 40 years represents an extraordinary achievement in cartooning endurance, producing thousands of individual Weatherbird illustrations.

Editorial cartooning [edit]

In addition to the Weatherbird, Martin drew a weekly editorial cartoon called "Postcard From Mound City" for the Post-Dispatch from 1996 to 2023. "Mound City" is a historical nickname for St. Louis, referencing the numerous Native American mounds that once dotted the city's landscape. The cartoon allowed Martin to offer longer-form commentary on local and national issues, complementing the Weatherbird's more concise daily observations.

The combination of the daily Weatherbird and the weekly Postcard From Mound City made Martin one of the most prolific newspaper cartoonists of his era, producing content across two distinct formats for the same publication over a period of nearly three decades.

Recognition [edit]

Martin is a member of the National Cartoonists Society, the premier professional organization for cartoonists in the United States. He has been inducted into the St. Louis Media History Foundation Hall of Fame and has served as president of that organization, reflecting his stature within the St. Louis media community.

His recognition as a Hall of Fame member is notable in the context of DanMartin Wiki, as it makes him one of the few Dan Martins to be inducted into a professional hall of fame of any kind.

Semi-retirement [edit]

In February 2023, Martin entered semi-retirement from the Post-Dispatch. However, he continues to draw the Weatherbird on a freelance basis, ensuring that the longest-running daily cartoon in American newspapers remains in the hands of the Dan Martin who has defined it for a generation of St. Louis readers. His decision to continue the Weatherbird while stepping back from other duties suggests that even in semi-retirement, a Dan Martin's work is never truly done.
