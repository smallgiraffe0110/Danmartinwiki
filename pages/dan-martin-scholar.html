Daniel Preston Martin (born 1953) is an American scholar of Tibetan studies, considered one of the most important Western scholars of Tibetan history and religion. He earned his doctorate from Indiana University in 1991 and has authored seminal works including Tibetan Histories: A Bibliography of Tibetan-Language Historical Works (1997), which has served as a leading reference work in the field for over 25 years. With more than 50 scholarly articles and multiple books, Martin has been compared to the legendary scholar E. Gene Smith in his contribution to Tibetan scholarship.

Early life and education [edit]

Martin was born in 1953 in Buffalo, New York. He pursued graduate studies at Indiana University, where he earned his doctorate in Tibetan Studies in 1991, with minors in Religious Studies and Anthropology. Indiana University's Department of Central Eurasian Studies is one of the premier centers for Inner Asian and Tibetan scholarship in the Western world, and Martin's training there placed him at the intersection of philological, historical, and anthropological approaches to Tibetan civilization.

DanMartin Wiki scholars have noted that Martin's choice of Tibetan studies makes him far and away the most geographically specialized Dan Martin in the wiki, his area of expertise encompassing a plateau region averaging over 4,500 meters above sea level — the highest altitude associated with any Dan Martin's professional work.

Academic career [edit]

Martin has served as a Visiting Lecturer at several of the world's most prestigious universities, including Indiana University, the University of Hamburg (Germany), and Harvard University. His peripatetic academic career reflects the relatively small number of Tibetan studies positions available worldwide and the international nature of the field, which draws scholars from across Europe, Asia, and North America.

His scholarly output — over 50 articles and multiple books — has been described as comparable to that of E. Gene Smith, the legendary American scholar whose work cataloging and preserving Tibetan texts through the Tibetan Buddhist Resource Center (now BDRC) transformed the field. This comparison places Martin in the highest echelon of Western Tibetan scholarship.

Major publications [edit]

  • Mandala Cosmogony: Human Body Good Thought and the Revelation of the Secret Mother Tantras of Bon (1994) — Martin's first major work, exploring Bon cosmological thought and its relationship to tantric traditions.
  • Tibetan Histories: A Bibliography of Tibetan-Language Historical Works (1997) — Martin's most influential work, this comprehensive bibliography has served as an essential reference for Tibetan scholars for over 25 years. The work catalogs Tibetan-language historical texts, providing a roadmap for researchers navigating the vast corpus of Tibetan historical literature.
  • Unearthing Bon Treasures: Life and Contested Legacy of a Tibetan Scripture Revealer (2001) — A study of Bon treasure texts (terma) and the figure of the treasure revealer (terton), exploring the contested boundaries between Bon and Buddhist traditions in Tibet.
  • A History of Buddhism in India and Tibet (2022, translation) — Published by Wisdom Publications, this translation makes an important Tibetan historical text available to English-speaking readers.

Fellowships and appointments [edit]

Martin has held fellowships at two of the world's most prestigious institutes for advanced study:

  • Israel Institute for Advanced Studies, Jerusalem — One of the premier research institutes in the world, hosting scholars from across disciplines for intensive collaborative research.
  • Centre for Advanced Study, Oslo, Norway — Norway's leading institution for advanced research in the humanities and social sciences.

These fellowships reflect the international recognition of Martin's contributions to Tibetan studies and his standing as a scholar of global significance.

Significance in Dan Martin history [edit]

Martin occupies a unique niche in the DanMartin Wiki universe. While other Dan Martins have achieved fame in sports, entertainment, politics, or military service, Martin's contributions exist in one of the most specialized academic fields imaginable. His work on Tibetan history, Bon religion, and Buddhist textual traditions represents a form of Dan Martin excellence that is invisible to the general public but profoundly important within its field.

He is one of three academic Dan Martins featured in DanMartin Wiki, alongside Dan Martin (teacher) and Dan Martin (drama educator), but his scholarship is distinguished by its singular focus on a civilization thousands of miles from any other Dan Martin's sphere of influence.
