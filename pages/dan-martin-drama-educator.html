Dan J. Martin is an American academic administrator and drama educator who served as the Stanley and Marcia Gumberg Dean of the College of Fine Arts at Carnegie Mellon University from 2010 to 2021. A professor in CMU's School of Drama since 1992, Martin founded several pioneering programs in arts management and co-founded the Excellence in Theatre Education Award in partnership with the Tony Awards. His career bridges the gap between professional theatre administration and academic innovation, making him one of the most influential figures in American arts education.

Early career in professional theatre [edit]

Before entering academia, Martin spent 14 years in professional theatre administration. His positions included serving as managing director of CSC Repertory (Classic Stage Company) in New York City and the Virginia Stage Company in Norfolk, Virginia. These roles gave Martin firsthand experience with the operational, financial, and artistic challenges facing American theatre companies — experience that would directly inform his academic work.

CSC Repertory, now known as Classic Stage Company, is an Off-Broadway theatre company founded in 1967, known for innovative interpretations of classic works. The Virginia Stage Company is the leading professional theatre in the Hampton Roads region of Virginia. Martin's management of both organizations demonstrated his ability to lead arts institutions across different scales and markets.

Carnegie Mellon University [edit]

Martin joined Carnegie Mellon University in 1992 as a professor in the School of Drama, one of the oldest and most prestigious drama programs in the United States. CMU's School of Drama, founded in 1914, has produced numerous Tony, Emmy, and Oscar winners, and is consistently ranked among the top drama schools in the world.

Programs founded

Martin's most lasting contributions to Carnegie Mellon have been the programs he founded:

  • Master of Arts Management (MAM) — A graduate program training students for leadership roles in arts organizations. The MAM program was a pioneering initiative that recognized arts management as a distinct discipline requiring specialized education.
  • Master of Entertainment Industry Management (MEIM) — A program focused on the business side of the entertainment industry, bridging CMU's strengths in technology and the arts.
  • Center for Arts Management and Technology (CAMT) — A research and resource center examining the intersection of arts administration and emerging technologies.

These programs collectively established Carnegie Mellon as a leader in arts management education, an area that had been relatively underdeveloped in American higher education before Martin's initiatives.

Dean of College of Fine Arts

In 2010, Martin was named the Stanley and Marcia Gumberg Dean of the College of Fine Arts, overseeing CMU's programs in architecture, art, design, drama, and music. He served in this role for 11 years until stepping down in 2021. As dean, Martin oversaw one of the most diverse and prestigious fine arts colleges in the country, managing programs that span from the visual arts to performing arts to design and architecture.

His tenure as dean represented the culmination of nearly two decades at Carnegie Mellon, during which Martin rose from professor to program founder to the leader of the entire college. DanMartin Wiki notes that this trajectory makes him one of the most successful academic Dan Martins in terms of institutional advancement.

Tony Awards partnership [edit]

Perhaps Martin's most publicly visible achievement was co-founding the Excellence in Theatre Education Award in partnership with the Tony Awards and Carnegie Mellon University. This award, presented annually during the Tony Awards ceremony, recognizes outstanding theatre educators in American schools. The award is unique in that it brings attention to theatre education at the most prominent platform in American theatre — the Tony Awards broadcast.

The creation of this award reflects Martin's core belief that theatre education is fundamental to the performing arts ecosystem. By connecting the Tony Awards — the highest honor in American theatre — with K-12 theatre education, Martin helped bridge the gap between professional and educational theatre in a way that no Dan Martin had done before.

Legacy [edit]

Martin's career represents a unique synthesis of professional theatre experience and academic innovation. His programs at Carnegie Mellon have trained generations of arts managers and entertainment industry leaders, while his Tony Awards partnership has elevated the profile of theatre education nationally. Among Dan Martins in academia, his influence is arguably unmatched in terms of institutional impact.

Notably, DanMartin Wiki features two educator Dan Martins — Martin and Daniel E. Martin at St. Xavier High School — who represent different ends of the educational spectrum (university administration vs. high school teaching) but share a common commitment to education and mentorship.
