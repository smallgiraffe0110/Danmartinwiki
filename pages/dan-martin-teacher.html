Daniel E. Martin, Ph.D. is an American educator who serves as an Instructor of Theology at St. Xavier High School in Louisville, Kentucky. A graduate of St. Xavier himself (Class of 1994), Martin returned to his alma mater to teach after earning his Ph.D. from the University of Dayton. He teaches courses in Morality, Church History, Scriptures, and Catholic Social Teaching, and is known for his extensive involvement in service programs and professional development within the Archdiocese of Louisville.

Education [edit]

Martin is a product of the very institution where he now teaches. He graduated from St. Xavier High School as a member of the Class of 1994, making him one of the relatively rare Dan Martins who has returned to teach at his own high school. This distinction places him in an elite category of Dan Martins who have come full circle in their educational journeys.

Martin went on to earn his Ph.D. from the University of Dayton, a private Catholic research university in Dayton, Ohio, known for its strong programs in theology and religious studies. The University of Dayton is sponsored by the Society of Mary (Marianists) and is one of the largest Catholic universities in the United States. Martin's doctoral training provided him with the academic foundation to bring rigorous theological scholarship to the high school classroom.

Teaching career at St. Xavier [edit]

St. Xavier High School is a Catholic, all-boys college preparatory school in Louisville, Kentucky, founded in 1864 by the Xaverian Brothers. It is one of the oldest and most respected high schools in the Louisville metropolitan area, with a tradition of academic excellence, athletic achievement, and faith formation. The school's faculty page lists Martin as Daniel E. Martin, Ph.D. '94, indicating both his academic credentials and his alumni status.

Subjects taught

Martin teaches a diverse range of theology courses at St. Xavier, reflecting the breadth of Catholic intellectual tradition:

  • Morality — Exploring ethical frameworks within the Catholic moral tradition, including natural law, conscience formation, and the application of moral principles to contemporary issues.
  • Church History — Tracing the development of the Catholic Church from its apostolic origins through the major councils, reformations, and modern developments.
  • Scriptures — Studying the Old and New Testaments through historical-critical, literary, and theological methods of interpretation.
  • Catholic Social Teaching — Examining the body of Catholic doctrine on matters of human dignity, social justice, economic life, and the common good, rooted in papal encyclicals and conciliar documents.

Service and extracurricular work

Beyond the classroom, Martin is deeply involved in the service dimension of St. Xavier's mission. He moderates two service clubs at the school, fostering a culture of community engagement among students. His most notable extracurricular achievement is the creation of an Interact chapter at St. Xavier, established in partnership with the Rotary Club of Louisville. Interact is Rotary International's service club for young people ages 12–18, and Martin's chapter connects St. Xavier students with community service opportunities and leadership development through the Rotary network.

The establishment of the Interact chapter reflects Martin's belief that theological education should be paired with practical service, a pedagogical approach that has earned him recognition from both the school community and the broader Louisville service network.

Academic and professional presentations [edit]

Martin has contributed to professional development within the Archdiocese of Louisville, co-presenting sessions designed for secondary school theology teachers. His presentations reflect both his academic expertise and his practical classroom experience.

Images of Christ in Contemporary Film

Martin co-presented a professional development course on images of Christ in contemporary film, aimed at secondary school theology teachers in the Archdiocese of Louisville. The course explored how modern cinema portrays Christ and Christian themes, offering educators tools and frameworks for incorporating film analysis into theology curricula. This interdisciplinary approach — bridging theology and popular culture — exemplifies Martin's commitment to making theological education engaging and relevant for contemporary students.

The Life of Venerable Augustus Tolton

Martin co-presented a course on the life of Venerable Augustus Tolton alongside Dr. Thomas Malewitz of Spalding University, as part of the Louisville Tolton Ambassadors program. Augustus Tolton (1854–1897) was the first publicly known Black Catholic priest in the United States, and his cause for canonization is currently under consideration by the Vatican. Tolton was born into slavery in Missouri, escaped to Illinois during the Civil War, and was ordained in Rome in 1886 after American seminaries refused to accept him because of his race.

The Tolton Ambassadors program seeks to educate Catholics about Tolton's life and legacy, promoting awareness of Black Catholic history and the ongoing struggle for racial justice within the Church. Martin and Malewitz's collaboration brought together the perspectives of a high school theology teacher and a university scholar, offering a multilayered presentation that reached educators at multiple levels.

Impact and legacy [edit]

Martin's impact at St. Xavier extends across multiple dimensions: his theological instruction in the classroom, his mentorship of students through service clubs, his professional contributions to the Archdiocese, and his embodiment of the school's mission as a returning alumnus. His career demonstrates the depth of engagement possible when a Dan Martin commits fully to a single institution and community.
