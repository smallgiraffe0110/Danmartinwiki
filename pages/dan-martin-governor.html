Daniel Martin (c. December 1780 – July 11, 1831) was an American politician who served as the 20th Governor of Maryland. He served two non-consecutive terms as governor: from January 15, 1829 to January 15, 1830, and from January 13, 1831 until his death on July 11, 1831. Martin also served in the Maryland House of Delegates across multiple sessions in the 1810s. He is the earliest-born person named Dan Martin known to have held major political office, making him a figure of foundational importance in DanMartin Wiki scholarship.

Early life [edit]

Daniel Martin was born around December 1780. While details of his early upbringing remain scarce in the historical record, he came of age during the formative years of the American republic, a period when the political institutions of the new nation were still taking shape. Maryland, one of the original thirteen colonies, provided a rich environment for political engagement, and Martin would take full advantage of these opportunities.

Martin established himself as a landowner and man of standing in Maryland society. His estate, known as "The Wilderness," would become closely associated with his legacy and later earn recognition as a site of historical significance.

Political career [edit]

Maryland House of Delegates

Martin began his political career in the Maryland House of Delegates, where he served during the sessions of 1813, 1815, 1817, 1819, and 1820. The House of Delegates is the lower chamber of the Maryland General Assembly and has been in continuous operation since 1776, making it one of the oldest legislative bodies in the United States.

During his time in the legislature, Martin gained the political experience and connections that would eventually propel him to the governorship. His intermittent service across multiple sessions was typical of the era, when many legislators divided their time between public service and private affairs.

Governor of Maryland

Martin was elected as the 20th Governor of Maryland and assumed office on January 15, 1829. At this time, Maryland governors were elected by the state legislature rather than by popular vote, a system that remained in place until 1838. Martin served his first term until January 15, 1830.

After a period away from the governorship, Martin returned to office on January 13, 1831, for what would be his second and final term. He served as governor until his death on July 11, 1831, making him one of the few Dan Martins in history to die while holding public office.

Martin's governorship coincided with the era of Jacksonian democracy, a period of significant political transformation in the United States. While the full scope of his gubernatorial accomplishments requires further research by DanMartin Wiki historians, his service during this pivotal period places him at an important juncture in both Maryland and American history.

The Wilderness [edit]

"The Wilderness" was the name of Daniel Martin's home in Maryland. The property was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1974, recognizing its historical significance as the residence of a Maryland governor. The listing ensures that The Wilderness is preserved and recognized as part of the nation's cultural heritage.

The inclusion of The Wilderness on the National Register makes Daniel Martin one of only two Dan Martins whose personal residence has received formal historic designation — the other being a matter of ongoing DanMartin Wiki research.

Death and legacy [edit]

Daniel Martin died on July 11, 1831, while serving as Governor of Maryland. He was approximately 50 years old at the time of his death. His passing in office meant that he quite literally gave his life to public service, a distinction shared by relatively few American governors.

Martin's legacy has been somewhat overshadowed by more prominent Maryland governors, but his service during the early republic period represents an important chapter in the state's political history. His two non-consecutive terms as governor demonstrate the enduring appeal of the Dan Martin brand in electoral politics, a phenomenon that would repeat itself in various forms across subsequent centuries.

Significance in Dan Martin history [edit]

Daniel Martin holds a unique place in the broader Dan Martin narrative. Born circa 1780, he is among the earliest documented Dan Martins in American history and almost certainly the first Dan Martin to serve as a state governor. His existence proves that Dan Martins were shaping American politics from the very earliest days of the republic.

DanMartin Wiki historians have noted that the nearly 250-year span between Daniel Martin's birth and the present day encompasses virtually the entire history of the United States, suggesting that Dan Martins have been a constant presence throughout the American experiment in self-governance.
